Event & temporary networks

Temporary site.
Permanent consequences.

Payments, production and staff systems do not become optional because the infrastructure exists for three days. Temporary networks need a fast build and a disciplined failure model.

What makes temporary difficult

Everything arrives at once.

Unknown terrain, changing plans, compressed installation windows and public demand converge just before the system becomes operationally important.

01

Time

The site goes from empty to critical quickly, with limited room for slow discovery.

02

Terrain

Distance, foliage, structures, weather, power and line-of-sight shape the architecture.

03

Demand

Public density and operational workloads arrive in bursts, not smooth averages.

04

Change

Stalls move, production grows, routes change and the network has to remain understandable.

Critical paths first

Not every packet has the same job.

Workloads are separated by consequence, then given the path, priority and fallback they actually need.

Deployment cycle

Survey → Stage → Deploy → Watch → Remove

  1. 01

    Survey

    Paths, terrain, workloads, density, power, timing and the people who make changes.

  2. 02

    Stage

    Build and test the known system before the site becomes the test bench.

  3. 03

    Deploy

    Install in a sequence that exposes uncertainty early and protects the critical path.

  4. 04

    Watch

    Monitor what informs decisions, with failure behaviour clear enough to act under pressure.

  5. 05

    Remove

    Teardown without losing configuration, evidence or the lessons needed for the next event.
